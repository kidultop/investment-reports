研究者 Warstadt、Sokol、Frank 等人认为 LLM 已成为「语言学习实验室替代品」，可以剥夺双语暴露、模拟特定语法缺失来验证语言习得假设——这反过来可能帮助低资源语言（捷克语、挪威语、Sami 语）补齐与英语 LLM 的能力差距；David Samuel 称 Sami 语可用训练 token 仅约几千万，「接近一个幼儿的语言暴露量」。

MIT Technology Review's August 24 deep dive surveys BabyLM and the NanoGPT Slowrun data-efficiency challenges: children acquire language with orders of magnitude less data than LLMs and still win. Meta appears the most interested, partnering with academics on a benchmark that trains models from baby headcam footage.

Researchers including Warstadt, Frank and Samuel argue LLMs are now usable as "linguistic lab rats" — simulating bilingual exposure or grammatical deprivation. That same lens could close capability gaps for low-resource languages like Czech, Norwegian and Sami.

为何重要 / Why it matters

Data efficiency is being recast as the core research question for the next decade — closing this gap could break the "more GPUs = better models" paradigm. It now reads alongside Hugging Face Delta Weight Sync and small-Agent training frameworks as a defining H2 2026 research thread.

arXiv's August 21 paper "RAG Deserves an Index" proposes pre-compiling atomic, provenance-validated claims at ingest time instead of re-deriving meaning from chunks per query. Compiled claims hit 85.2% correctness at ~2.2k tokens versus 72.5% at 16.3k tokens for chunk-based RAG.

Companion paper "Cost Scales with Change, Not Corpus Size" shows incremental SVD updates are 33.7x cheaper than full reindexing. Other 17–22 Aug papers include AgenticRAG-FP (post-hoc diagnosis collapses past the first hop), MemStrata and VisDocAgentBench.
